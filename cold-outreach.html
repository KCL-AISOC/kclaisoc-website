Cold Outreach, Explained

How to reach firms that never advertise, and turn a message into a meeting. In plain English.

04

The 30 second version

  • Big firms hire through schemes, so cold outreach works best on small firms, boutiques and search funds.
  • Find them on LinkedIn by filtering for location, industry and a small company size, then research one at a time.
  • Reach out by email, by phone, or through a warm LinkedIn connection, and always make it personal and short.
01 Section 1

Why cold outreach works

Large firms recruit through structured programmes, so a cold email to a big bank usually goes nowhere. Smaller firms are different. Many boutiques, search funds and alternative investment shops have no formal scheme at all. They hire when they meet someone good. That is exactly where a sharp, personal message can create an opportunity that was never posted on any job board. So the whole game is to target small.

02 Section 2

Find the firms

Use LinkedIn to build a list of small firms in the area you care about. Work through it one firm at a time.

Go to LinkedIn, search a term, then switch the results filter to Companies.

Filter Location to United Kingdom, then narrow to London.

Filter Industry to the area you want, for example investment banking, private equity or asset management.

Filter Company size to small, choosing 1 to 10 employees and 11 to 50 employees. Small firms are where cold outreach actually lands.

Work through the list one firm at a time, rather than blasting everyone at once.

03 Section 3

Vet the firm first

Before you reach out, open the firm's About section on LinkedIn and read it properly. Ask yourself one question: does what they do match what I am interested in? If yes, go to their website to confirm and to find a way in. If no, move on. A few well researched messages beat fifty generic ones.

04 Section 4

Three ways in

There are three channels. Pick the strongest one available for each firm.

Option A

The general email

Find the contact address on the website, often an info or careers inbox. Send a short, personal, well aimed email. This is the easiest to do but has the lowest reply rate.

Easiest to find. Lowest reply rate.

Option B

The direct approach

Find a specific senior person, ideally a partner, on the website or LinkedIn. If their email is listed, write to them directly. If a phone number is listed, even better. Call during business hours with a prepared 60 second pitch. Going direct to a decision maker gets the best response.

More effort. Best response rate.

Option C

The warm LinkedIn route

Look for people you are already connected with, or who share a connection, who work there. Connect, then ask for a short coffee chat or a quick online call. Use it to learn and build rapport, then gradually raise the idea of getting involved or working with them. This starts from a real connection, so it has the highest hit rate.

Starts from a real connection. Highest hit rate.

05 Section 5

How to write the cold email

Keep it under 150 words and make every line earn its place. Use this structure.

Short and specific, so it gets opened.

One line on who you are.

One specific, genuine detail about the firm, which proves you did your research.

Make it clear and small, for example a short call or whether they ever take on students.

Thank them and make it easy to say yes.

Sample email

Subject: KCL student interested in [Firm name]

Hi [First name],

I am [your name], a [year and course] student at King's College London and a member of our Alternative Investment Society. I came across [Firm name] while researching London firms in [the area, for example private credit], and I was drawn to [one specific genuine detail about the firm].

I would love to learn how you built [Firm name], and whether you ever take on students for short projects or work experience. Would you be open to a short call in the next couple of weeks?

Thank you for your time.

Best wishes,
[Your name]
[Your LinkedIn]

06 Section 6

The 60 second phone pitch

If you call, be ready before you dial. Call during business hours, be polite and brief, and keep your script in front of you. Cover who you are, why you are calling with one specific reason, what you are asking for, and a thank you.

Sample script

Hi [First name], my name is [your name]. I am a student at King's College London and I help run our Alternative Investment Society. I am calling because I have been following [Firm name]'s work in [area] and I am keen to learn from firms like yours. I am not asking for a job. I would love fifteen minutes of your time to hear how you got started, and whether there is any way a motivated student could help out. Would you have a moment this week or next?

07 Section 7

Follow up without being annoying

One follow up after about a week is fine. Two at most. Keep it short and friendly, and if there is still no reply, move on. No reply is rarely personal, people are simply busy. Persistence is good, pestering is not.

08 Section 8

Dos and don'ts

Do

  • Personalise every message.
  • Target small firms.
  • Be specific about why them.
  • Keep it short.
  • Follow up once.
  • Be polite and grateful.

Don't

  • Send the same email to everyone.
  • Target huge firms that only hire through schemes.
  • Write long essays.
  • Attach large files no one asked for.
  • Sound desperate.
  • Give up after a single try.

10 Section 10

The bottom line

Cold outreach rewards effort and research. The students who get somewhere are the ones who target small, do their homework, and make a real human ask. Done well, it opens doors that were never officially open.
