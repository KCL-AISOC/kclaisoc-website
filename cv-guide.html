CV Guide

How to structure your CV for alternative investment and finance roles.

This guide covers bullet-writing technique, how to order each section, formatting rules, and a full example CV you can use as a reference alongside the checklist.

CV
The Foundation

The Golden Rule

Recruiters spend seconds on each CV, so every bullet has to land on a single line. That limit does more work than any other rule here: it forces out the filler and leaves only the result. Inside that line, use the formula made famous by Google. State what you achieved, prove it with a number, explain how you did it.

Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by doing [Z].

X
The accomplishment or result

What changed because you were there. What did you build, improve, or deliver?

Y
The measure

A number, percentage, value, rank, or timeframe that proves the scale of your impact.

Z
The action

The specific skill, method, or decision you used to get there.

Length

One line, every time. If a bullet wraps onto a second line, cut words until it fits. XYZ decides what goes in. The line limit decides what survives.

Examples

Before

Took part in a trading simulation and performed well.

→
After

Returned 6% ($1.2m) on a $20m multi-asset book across three simulated quarters.

Before

Helped students with their maths and economics work.

→
After

Raised attainment 18% across 19 tutees by regressing session hours against results.

Quick checklist for every bullet

  • Does it open with a strong action verb (Led, Built, Generated, Designed)?
  • Does it fit on a single line?
  • Is there at least one number proving the scale or result?
  • Is the how clear, not just the what?
  • Have you cut filler words such as responsible for, helped with, and various?
  • Have you tailored at least one bullet to something specific about this firm or role?
  • Have you used a different action verb from every other bullet?

Struggling to get a bullet down to one line? Copy our prompt, paste it into Claude with your draft, and it will apply every rule on this page.

How to Structure Your CV

01

Header

  • Name, city, phone, email and LinkedIn on one line
  • No photo, no personal statement, no street address
  • Make LinkedIn a live link and check where it points
  • A copied template often still links to whoever made it
02

Education

  • University first, with the degree and grade on the same line
  • Abbreviate the degree if that is what makes it fit
  • Modules with marks below, strongest mark first
  • A Levels underneath, societies belong in extracurriculars
03

Work Experience

  • Most recent first, full bullets for your strongest three or four roles
  • List the rest on one line as Additional Insight Programmes
  • Cap that line at four firms, strongest first
  • Put selection ratios in the role header, never inside a bullet
04

Extracurriculars

  • Societies, competitions and personal projects, same bullet rules
  • What you built outranks what you joined
  • Keep every figure identical to your application forms
  • They get read side by side
05

Skills & Interests

  • Technical skills with honest levels
  • Languages, plus any access programme that selected you
  • Interests specific enough that someone could ask about them
  • Cut anything you would not defend in an interview
06

Format

  • One page, one font, past tense throughout
  • Never reuse an action verb
  • No tables or text boxes, they break ATS parsing
  • Margins 1.5cm minimum, nothing below 9pt, export to PDF

Spring weeks, insight programmes and internships belong here, most recent first. Selection ratios, "1 of 4 selected", "1 of 12 accepted", "1 of 50 selected", are evidence before the work even starts. Group the minor insights onto one line rather than padding the section.

5

Skills, Languages & Interests

5 Honest Skills

Honest levels, Python (Intermediate) rather than a bare "Python", read better, because interviewers probe what you claim. Access schemes signal you seek out opportunity; a short interests line gives the conversation somewhere to go.
